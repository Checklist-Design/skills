# Feedback — Mobile app

In-app prompts that ask users for their opinion — NPS surveys, rating scales, and open text — surfaced at the right moment and at the right frequency.

Source: https://www.checklist.design/mobile/feedback

## Items

### Prompt timing
The moment at which the feedback request appears, chosen to follow a point of completed value rather than a fixed session count

_Tip: The highest-quality feedback comes from users who have just done something successfully, since the experience is fresh and the sentiment is accurate. Asking after a failure, or on launch, produces noise_

### Contextual trigger
The specific in-app event or screen that initiates the feedback request, relevant to the question being asked

_Tip: 'How easy was it to complete that payment?' asked immediately after checkout produces better signal than 'How do you feel about the app?' asked on the home screen_

### Single-question entry
Starting the feedback flow with one question rather than a form, to lower the barrier to responding

_Tip: NPS surveys convert because they start with one question. Even if more follow, the initial commitment to a single answer gets users through the door, since the drop-off at step one is the most significant_

### Rating scale
A numeric scale (typically 0–10 for NPS or 1–5 for CSAT) as the primary feedback signal

_Tip: Segment the follow-up question by score range. Promoters, passives, and detractors have different things to say, since a single follow-up question for all three produces averaged, less useful responses_

### Open text follow-up
An optional text field shown after the rating, inviting the user to expand on their score.

_Tip: Mark it explicitly as optional and say so. 'Tell us more (optional)' outperforms an unlabelled empty field — users need to know they can skip before they will commit to the rating step._

### Dismissal option
A clear way to close the prompt without responding.

_Tip: Label the dismiss action neutrally — 'Not now' rather than 'No thanks'. The user who dismisses today is still worth asking again in three months._

### Frequency cap
A limit on how often the same user sees a feedback request, regardless of how many trigger conditions are met.

_Tip: Users who dismiss a prompt repeatedly are signalling disinterest — continued prompts push them toward frustration rather than response. Once per session is a reasonable maximum; once per month a reasonable minimum interval._

### Submission confirmation
A brief acknowledgement that the feedback was received before returning the user to their previous context.

_Tip: A one-line thank you that disappears after two seconds is enough. What matters is that the user knows it landed — without confirmation, some will submit again assuming the first attempt failed._
